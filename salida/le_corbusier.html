Le Corbusier

1917-1965

Born Charles-Édouard Jeanneret-Gris in Switzerland in 1887, Le Corbusier became one of modern architecture's greatest pioneers despite having no formal training in the field. Moving to Paris in 1917, he revolutionized 20th-century design by combining bold sculptural expressionism with practical functionalism, developing influential principles like the Five Points of Architecture that transformed how buildings were constructed. Passionate about improving urban living, he shaped cities from Paris to India's Chandigarh while also crafting iconic furniture pieces like the LC4 chaise longue. His legacy spans architecture, urban planning, painting, and writing—a tireless visionary who believed good design could create better lives for everyone. Today, seventeen of his projects across seven countries stand as UNESCO World Heritage Sites, celebrating his immense contribution to the Modern Movement.
