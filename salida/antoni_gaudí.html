Antoni Gaudí

1852-1926 (active 1878-1926)

Born in 1852 to a coppersmith family in Catalonia, Antoni Gaudí became the visionary architect who transformed Catalan Modernisme into something entirely his own—a revolutionary organic style inspired by nature itself. Drawing from neo-Gothic art, Oriental techniques, and the natural world, he developed groundbreaking methods like trencadís, creating beauty from broken ceramic pieces. His deepening Catholic faith earned him the nickname "God's Architect," leading him to devote the last 43 years of his life to the magnificent Sagrada Família, eventually living on its construction site. Gaudí's buildings seemed to grow rather than stand, designed like trees that needed no internal bracing—a radical departure from conventional architecture. Though a trolley accident cut his life short in 1926, his legacy endures as one of history's most beloved and innovative architects, recently recognized as Venerable by Pope Francis.
