Zaha Hadid

1980-2016

Born in Baghdad in 1950, Zaha Hadid brought a mathematician's mind to architecture, studying at London's prestigious Architectural Association before launching her own practice in 1980. Her 1983 competition-winning design for The Peak in Hong Kong announced a bold new style—deconstructivism—with its sharp angles, fragmentation, and daring forms that challenged conventional architecture. As technology evolved, so did her vision; she became famous as the "Queen of Curves" for her flowing, organic designs created through cutting-edge parametric software and innovative engineering. In 2004, she made history as the first woman to win the Pritzker Architecture Prize, and in 2012 was made a Dame for her transformative contributions to the field. Hadid's legacy—from her groundbreaking early concepts to her realized masterpieces—reshaped how architects think about space, form, and the future of building design.
