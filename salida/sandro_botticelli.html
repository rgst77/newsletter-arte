Sandro Botticelli

c. 1445-1510 (active 1465-1505)

Sandro Botticelli (c. 1445–1510) was one of Florence's greatest Renaissance painters, trained first as a goldsmith and later in the workshops of Filippo Lippi and Andrea del Verrocchio. As the favorite artist of the powerful Medici family, he became a pioneer in bringing classical mythology to life on canvas—his "Birth of Venus" was groundbreaking as the first major panel painting to depict a Greek mythological scene. Influenced by the Neoplatonic Academy's rediscovery of ancient philosophy, Botticelli's delicate, graceful style perfectly captured the Renaissance dream of blending organic beauty with mathematical precision. Later in life, he turned toward devotional subjects and created a remarkable 93 illustrations for Dante's Divine Comedy, leaving behind a legacy that would later inspire the Pre-Raphaelite artists who championed his work centuries after his death.
