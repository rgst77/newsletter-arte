Modernism · 1879-1905

Camille Claudel

Camille Claudel was a French sculptor who broke through the gender barriers of her time, studying at the progressive Académie Colarossi when the official École des Beaux-Arts was closed to women. Working alongside Auguste Rodin for about a decade, she developed a distinctive artistic voice that captured profound human emotion in bronze and marble, with a style that was both sensuous and powerfully expressive. Her figurative sculptures explored deep psychological themes and the complexities of human experience, earning her recognition as an innovative and influential artist of the modernist movement. Though her career was tragically cut short when mental health struggles led to her institutionalization from 1905 onward, her legacy has been beautifully reclaimed and celebrated, with around 90 of her remarkable works surviving today. The 2017 opening of the Musée Camille Claudel stands as a testament to her enduring artistic significance and creative brilliance.
