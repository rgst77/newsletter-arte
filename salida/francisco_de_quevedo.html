Francisco de Quevedo

1580-1645

Francisco de Quevedo (1580–1645) was a Spanish nobleman and one of the Baroque era's most dazzling writers, mastering a witty, sharp style called conceptismo that played with words and ideas like a verbal magician. Orphaned young and educated by Jesuits, he became a poet, satirist, and prose writer whose clever wordplay and satirical bite earned him praise from literary giants like Cervantes and Lope de Vega. His work spanned everything from passionate sonnets to biting satire, all packed with the kind of intellectual cleverness that makes you smile and think at the same time. Though he spent much of his life at court and even served as secretary to the king, Quevedo's real legacy is as the master of conceptismo—the perfect antidote to flowery, excessive language, proving that sometimes the sharpest wit comes in the smallest package.
