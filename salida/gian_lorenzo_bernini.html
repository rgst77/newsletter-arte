Gian Lorenzo Bernini

1598-1680

Born in Naples in 1598, Gian Lorenzo Bernini became the defining artistic voice of the Baroque era and arguably the greatest sculptor of the 17th century. Recognized as a prodigy from childhood by his father, a Mannerist sculptor, he quickly won the patronage of powerful cardinals and Popes, including Urban VIII and Innocent X, who kept him at the heart of Rome's artistic life. Bernini revolutionized sculpture itself, transforming it from static form into theatrical, emotionally charged works that seemed to pulse with life, movement, and dramatic light—creating an entirely new visual language for the Baroque. Working across sculpture, architecture, painting, and city planning, he shaped not just individual masterpieces but the very look of Rome and the aesthetic sensibilities of all Europe. His legacy endured for centuries, making him one of history's most influential artists.
