Yūzō Saeki

1898-1928 (active 1918-1928)

Born in Osaka in 1898 as the son of a Buddhist priest, Yūzō Saeki showed an early passion for art that led him from studying Impressionism in Tokyo to becoming a pioneering figure in modern Japanese oil painting. Moving to Paris in 1924, he discovered his true voice through the vibrant colors and bold brushwork of Fauvist painters like Maurice de Vlaminck, creating stunning cityscapes of Parisian backstreets and bars that would influence generations to come. Though he returned briefly to Japan to co-found an artists' society and win recognition, he felt compelled to go back to Paris in 1927, painting outdoors even as tuberculosis ravaged his health. Saeki's meteoric career lasted just six years before his death in a mental hospital in 1928 at only 30 years old, yet his work secured his place as one of the most original voices of the École de Paris and a founding father of modern Japanese visual culture. His legacy lives on in the evocative urban landscapes that continue to inspire artists today.
