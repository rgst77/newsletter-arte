Tarsila do Amaral

1920-1973 (active modernist painter 1920s-1930s)

Tarsila do Amaral (1886–1973) was a Brazilian painter who became one of Latin America's most influential modernist artists, born into a wealthy coffee plantation family that gave her the freedom to pursue her artistic dreams. After studying Cubism in Paris and absorbing European avant-garde techniques, she did something revolutionary: she brought it all home and transformed it into something uniquely Brazilian. Co-founding the Anthropophagic movement alongside poet Oswald de Andrade, Tarsila pioneered a bold new idea—that Brazilian artists should "cannibalize" European influences, digesting and reinventing them into a distinctly Brazilian visual language. Her work across multiple periods, from the Pau-Brasil phase to social realism, gave modern Brazil its own visual identity and proved that modernism didn't have to copy Europe; it could become something entirely new. She remains a towering figure in Brazilian art, showing how cultural pride and artistic innovation can dance together beautifully.
